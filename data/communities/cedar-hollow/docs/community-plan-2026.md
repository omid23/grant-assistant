# Community plan 2026 to 2028 (Cedar Hollow)

This plan was approved by council in September 2026 and sets the work for the next two years. It is reviewed every six months.

## Plan items

The arena roof replacement is the largest single item, budgeted at $1.2 million for 2027, with a tender in March. Engineering found the trusses sound, so the work is the roof deck, insulation and new drainage only. The arena stays closed from April to September that year and the summer programs move to the school gym. The public works manager owns the project and reports to council after each milestone. Success is measured by reopening before the hockey season and by heating costs dropping in the first winter. The main water line from the plant to the east subdivision will be replaced in two phases over 2027 and 2028. Phase one covers the first 1.4 kilometres and is funded through the capital agreement. Phase two depends on a new application and is listed as unfunded for now. The water operator and the engineering firm share oversight, with weekly site visits during construction. Boil water notices on the east line should end once phase one is commissioned. The daycare expansion adds two rooms and sixteen spaces, with construction planned for summer 2027. Staffing for the new rooms needs three early childhood educators and a part time cook. The band office will apply for operating funding once the construction grant is confirmed. The daycare director leads the project with support from the housing coordinator on the building side. The waiting list, currently twenty two children, should clear within a year of opening. Home care hours for elders rise from twenty to thirty per week starting January 2027. Two more personal support workers will be hired in the fall and trained before the increase. Transport to medical appointments in town continues on Tuesdays and Thursdays. The health director owns this item and reviews the client list with the nurse every month. Fewer hospital transfers for elders is the measure council asked for. Twelve housing units are scheduled for repair in 2027 under the repair program, prioritising elders and families with young children. Four new duplexes are proposed for the north lots, pending a land designation vote in the spring. A housing coordinator position is funded for two years to run both streams. The housing committee approves each repair and the coordinator tracks spending against the $25,000 per home limit. Mould complaints and the repair backlog are reported to council twice a year. Road grading moves to a monthly schedule from May to October, and dust control is applied twice a season. The culvert at the creek crossing is replaced in August 2027 before the fall rains. Street lighting on the school road is extended by six poles. Public works owns the roads budget and logs every grading run. Complaints about dust and washouts are the measure, tracked through the band office. The youth centre opens on Saturdays from January 2027, adding eight hours a week. Two youth workers share the weekend shift, and the leadership program moves its sessions there. A small skills workshop space is set up in the old storage room. The program manager owns the centre and the youth council reviews the schedule each season. Attendance on Saturdays and the number of youth in the leadership program are reported quarterly. The language nest moves to the old school building in January and adds a second morning group. Three fluent speakers are contracted for recording sessions through the winter. Recordings are archived with the cultural centre and shared with the school. The language coordinator owns the nest and the recording project together. Hours of recorded speech and the number of children in the nest are the measures. The health centre front desk hire approved in September starts in November. Mental wellness drop in hours run twice a week with a counsellor from the regional authority. A diabetes clinic visits quarterly and the nurse runs follow ups between visits. The health director reports clinic numbers to the regional authority and to council. Wait times at the front desk and follow up completion are tracked monthly. The community garden expands by eight plots and a small tool shed in spring 2027. A greenhouse is listed as a future item once a funding source is found. Harvest sharing continues through the food bank on the first Friday of each month. The garden committee runs the plots with one summer student. Pounds of produce shared through the food bank is the measure council asked for. The backup generator at the band office is replaced in 2027 so the office can serve as a warming centre. Evacuation routes are reviewed with the regional emergency office in the spring. Emergency supply kits are refreshed and stored at the school and the arena. The band manager owns emergency planning and runs one tabletop exercise a year. A full power test of the warming centre happens every November. The finance office moves to monthly reporting to council on all grant funded projects. A records management review is planned to prepare for the new case management software. Staff training days are set for February and September. The finance manager owns reporting and the band manager owns the records review. Late grant reports to funders should fall to zero by the end of 2027. The summer gathering returns to the river site in July with camping for visiting families. A cultural camp for youth runs for one week in August with elders leading each day. The cultural centre extends its hours during the gathering week. The cultural coordinator owns both events with a volunteer committee. Visiting families registered and youth completing the camp are the measures. The school adds a land based learning day every second Friday starting in January. Bursaries for post secondary students are reviewed in June and paid in August. A tutoring program runs after school on Mondays and Wednesdays. The education director owns the school items and the bursary committee owns bursaries. Attendance on land based days and the number of students completing a term are reported. The gas bar renovation is complete and the convenience store adds fresh produce in 2027. A feasibility study for a small sawmill is funded and reports back by October. Two summer student positions are created in the band office and the store. The economic development officer owns the study and the store manager owns produce sales. Store revenue and local jobs created are reported to council quarterly. Notices move to the mobile app as the primary channel, with paper copies at the band office and the store. A monthly newsletter continues for households without phones. The website is refreshed with program pages and contact details. The communications officer owns all three channels. App sign ups and newsletter pickups are counted each month.

## Review

The next review is in March 2027. Changes to any budgeted item over $25,000 need a council motion.
